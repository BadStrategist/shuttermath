Focal Length Explained

Angles of view for 14–200 mm, what wide/normal/telephoto each do, and why perspective comes from where you stand — not your lens.

Focal length is the distance in millimetres between the lens's optical center and the sensor when focused at infinity. It sets one thing: angle of view. Short focal lengths show a wide scene; long ones magnify a narrow slice. Everything else about lens choice follows from that.

Angles computed with the standard formula AOV = 2·arctan(sensor width / (2·focal length)). On APS-C, Micro 4/3 or phones, the same lens frames differently — see the crop factor guide.

What each focal length is for

  • Ultra-wide (14–24 mm): space, drama, context. Great for real estate and astro, but easy to make empty scenes look empty.
  • Wide (24–35 mm): the travel/street range. Fits more story in frame while keeping a natural look.
  • Normal (50 mm): what your eye roughly sees. Unflattering to features up close, flattering at distance — the classic all-rounder.
  • Short tele (85–135 mm): the portrait range. Compresses features, flatters faces, and backgrounds blur beautifully.
  • Tele (135 mm+): reach and isolation for wildlife, sports, and candid shots.

The perspective myth

A wide lens doesn't "exaggerate" and a telephoto doesn't "compress" — distance does both. Photograph a face at 24 mm from 30 cm and at 135 mm from 3 m, then crop the wide shot to match the telephoto: the perspective is identical. Lenses only change what fits in the frame; where you stand changes the picture.

Focal length FAQ

What does focal length mean?

The mm distance from lens to sensor at infinity focus — it sets angle of view: shorter = wider, longer = more magnified.

What is a normal lens?

About 50 mm on full frame, 35 mm on APS-C — the natural, unexaggerated look.

Does focal length change perspective?

No. Distance changes perspective. Focal length only changes how much fits in the frame.

What is 35 mm equivalent?

Your lens's focal length on full frame, given the same field of view: multiply by your sensor's crop factor.
