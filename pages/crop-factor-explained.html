Crop Factor Explained: Full Frame, APS-C & M4/3 Equivalents | ShutterMath

What crop factor really means: focal length and aperture equivalence computed for full frame, APS-C, Micro 4/3 and phone cameras — and why exposure doesn't change.

Crop factor is the diagonal of a full-frame sensor (43.27 mm) divided by your sensor's diagonal. It answers one question: how does my lens frame and blur compared with the same lens on full frame?

Focal length equivalence (same framing)

Crop factor FAQ

What is crop factor?

Full-frame diagonal (43.27 mm) ÷ your sensor diagonal. APS-C ≈ 1.5×, Canon APS-C ≈ 1.6×, Micro 4/3 = 2×.

Does crop factor change exposure?

No — never. Same aperture, shutter and ISO = same exposure on any format. Crop factor only changes framing and depth of field.

Do lenses change focal length on crop sensors?

No. A 50 mm lens is always 50 mm. It just frames like a 75 mm would on full frame.
