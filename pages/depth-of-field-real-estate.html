Depth of Field for Real Estate Photography

Real estate is the opposite of portraits: you want everything sharp from the countertop to the back window. Good news — it's a solved math problem, and the answer is usually f/8.

The f/8 rule, with math

A 16 mm lens on full frame at f/8 has a hyperfocal distance of about 1.1 m — focus at 1.1 m and everything from 0.55 m to infinity is sharp. That covers an entire room. On APS-C, a 10–12 mm lens at f/8 is similar (~0.7–0.9 m hyperfocal). Get exact values in the hyperfocal calculator or the chart.

That's why f/8 is the real-estate default: f/5.6 risks a soft near edge, and f/11–f/16 buys almost nothing (the near limit barely moves) while adding diffraction. f/8 is the diffraction-free sweet spot with more than enough DoF.

Where to focus

The classic method: focus ⅓ into the room (not the back wall, not the floor). At hyperfocal it doesn't matter — focus at H and everything from H/2 to infinity is sharp. Practical workflow:

  1. Compose the room, set f/8, focus on a point about ⅓ of the way into the frame (a chair arm, the middle of a counter).
  2. If your lens has no distance scale (most mirrorless), focus-peak on something at the ⅓ point or use the hyperfocal number with the focus ring's readout.
  3. For large rooms or angled shots where the nearest element is closer than H/2, focus on the nearest element and stop to f/11 if needed — or take two focus-bracketed shots.

Technique details that matter

  • Tripod, always. At f/8 and ISO 100 you'll be at 1/4–1 s indoors — no handheld margin. Level the camera (bubble or electronic level) so verticals stay straight.
  • HDR/Exposure blending: windows blow out at any single exposure. Shoot 3–5 brackets (±2 EV) and blend — the DoF math is identical for every frame, so brackets stack cleanly.
  • Vertical framing for tight spaces (bathrooms): tilt shift? No — keep the camera level and stitch two vertical frames later. Tilting the camera up ruins verticals even if DoF still covers the room.
  • Flash or ambient? HDR ambient is the norm; flash adds punch but complicates DoF (flash falloff needs higher ISO or more power, not aperture changes).

Real estate DoF FAQ

Why does my real estate shot look sharp in the middle but soft at the edges?

Lens field curvature or corner softness, not DoF — wide lenses are softest at the edges wide open. Stop to f/8–f/11 (where the lens is sharpest) and check if the corners recover; if not, it's lens quality, and no aperture change fixes it.

Should I shoot f/11 to be safe?

Only if the nearest element is inside H/2. On full frame with a 16 mm lens that means closer than ~0.55 m — rare in a composed room shot. f/11's diffraction starts to soften overall detail for zero practical DoF gain. Use f/8 and a correct focus point.

Does a tilt-shift lens change the DoF math?

Yes — tilt changes the plane of focus, so a tilted lens can have a "wedge" of sharpness covering a floor-to-ceiling scene at f/5.6 where a regular lens would need f/8+. The hyperfocal numbers here apply to regular (non-tilted) lenses.
