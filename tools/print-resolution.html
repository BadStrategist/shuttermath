What DPI do you actually need?

  • 300 DPI — the fine-art standard for prints viewed up close (A4 and smaller).
  • 240 DPI — the typical photo-lab ceiling; most labs print at this and it's indistinguishable from 300 in practice.
  • 150 DPI — perfectly fine for large wall prints and canvases viewed from arm's length or more. A 24 MP camera prints 40×27 in at 150 DPI.
  • Below ~100 DPI — softness becomes visible at normal viewing distances; resize with care.

Print resolution FAQ

Can I print bigger than the calculator says?

Yes — software upscaling (or the lab's own upscaler) can print any size; the calculator shows the size at which every pixel maps cleanly to the DPI grid. Most labs upsample to 300 DPI automatically, and modern AI upscalers make modest enlargements invisible.

Is 300 DPI always better than 150 DPI?

For the same print size, higher DPI means more detail only if the viewing distance is close. A 40-inch print viewed from 6 feet at 150 DPI looks identical to 300 DPI to most eyes. Choose DPI by viewing distance, not by the maximum number.

What DPI should I use for a canvas or metal print?

Canvas: 150 DPI is the norm (the texture hides detail anyway). Metal and acrylic: 240–300 DPI recommended since they're glossy and often viewed close.
