Sharpest Aperture for Every Camera

Every lens has a sweet spot. Diffraction-limited apertures computed for real cameras: where the Airy disk meets your sensor's pixel pitch — and the practical f-stops to use.

Every lens has a sharpness sweet spot. Wide open, optical aberrations soften the edges; stop down and they improve — but eventually diffraction takes over and the image softens again. The balance point is different for every camera, and it's computable.

The Airy disk: where softness comes from

Light passing through a circular aperture doesn't focus to a point — it spreads into a blur disc called the Airy disk, diameter 2.44 × λ × N (λ ≈ 0.55 µm for green light). When that disk grows larger than your sensor's pixels, diffraction is visible at 100%:

Practical sweet spots

  • Portraits: f/1.8–f/2.8 — sharpness isn't the point; blur is.
  • Landscapes (tripod): f/8–f/11 — f/8 is the safe sweet spot, f/11 for extra DoF.
  • Real estate / interiors: f/8 — enough depth, still pixel-sharp.
  • Macro: stop down freely; focus stacking beats diffraction.

Diffraction FAQ

What is the sharpest aperture?

Usually f/4–f/8 on full frame. Past f/8–f/11 diffraction starts softening detail at the pixel level.

Is f/16 unusable?

No. At print sizes the softening is invisible; f/16 is fine when you need maximum depth of field and can't stack.

Do phone cameras diffract?

Yes, and much earlier — a 48 MP phone sensor is diffraction-limited around f/1.8–f/2. That's why phone lenses stay wide open.
