Worked example

You're shooting f/2.8 at 1/250 s, ISO 100 — the calculator reports EV ≈ 11. You want more depth of field: open to f/4 costs one stop of light, so the equivalent exposure becomes 1/125 s (one stop slower). Same EV 11, same brightness, twice the depth of field. That's the entire point of the tool: instead of guessing, set the aperture you want, read the shutter you need, and only reach for ISO when neither aperture nor shutter can move. In practice this kills the most common exposure error — changing one setting and forgetting to compensate the other.

Exposure value FAQ

What is exposure value (EV)?

EV = log₂(N²/t) — a single number describing how much light reaches the sensor for a given aperture N and shutter time t. EV100 is the EV at ISO 100. Higher EV = brighter scene or more light collected.

Do equivalent exposures look identical?

Brightness: yes. But each parameter changes the image: aperture changes depth of field, shutter changes motion blur, ISO changes noise. Equivalence lets you trade one for another while keeping brightness constant.

How does ISO fit into EV?

ISO doesn't change the light — it changes amplification. The scene-brightness value is EV100 − log₂(ISO/100): at ISO 400 (2 stops), the same scene reads as 2 EVs lower, which is why you can shoot at 1/4 the shutter speed or 2 stops narrower aperture.

Exposure value chart: what EV means in practice

Short answer: EV measures the light, not the camera settings. EV 15 is bright sun, EV 8 is a lit street at night, and each EV step is one stop of shutter, aperture or ISO. At ISO 100, EV = log2(N squared / t).

Read this as a sanity check. A grey card in bright sun sits at EV 15; snow and white sand need +1 to +2 EV of compensation because the meter wants to render them grey, and a dark forest or a backlit subject needs -1 EV.

One EV is one stop, so equivalent exposures share an EV: f/2.8 at 1/500 s, f/8 at 1/60 s and f/11 at 1/30 s are the same brightness. The calculator above converts between them without changing the exposure.
